Advanced search syntax

Strict (default): your words are required together in each paper's title/abstract — tight, on-topic results. Broad (Strict off): PubMed adds synonyms and MeSH — wider, but can drift.

Operators always work and override Strict: AND OR NOT (uppercase), "exact phrase", term[tiab] title/abstract, term[mesh], author[au], 2020[dp].

Example: glioblastoma AND temozolomide[tiab] NOT pediatric

Author and institution: pick from the list as you type, and OpenAlex finds exactly that person or place (an institution with its institutes). The other databases match the name as written, with the ORCID where they keep it, so they miss papers that spell it differently. A name you don't pick is searched as typed. Leave the search box empty to list everything by that author or institution. Databases that keep no authors or no institutions say so and are left out.

Languages: add one and each database is asked for English and that language only, English first. Those that can't be asked (Web of Science, Crossref, CORE, arXiv, IEEE Xplore, Semantic Scholar) say so, and leave out only the papers they say are in another language. Remove them all to search in any language again.

DOI: paste one or more DOIs, doi.org links or links to the articles, and each ticked database looks those papers up. Years, Strict, author, institution and languages don't apply; a paper none of them has is shown from Crossref.

Create a key at console.anthropic.com: sign in, add some credit under Billing (AI use is paid by the token), then Create Key and copy it.
Switch the AI on or off, and set a monthly spending limit, under AI in the bottom bar.
Open your NCBI account settings: sign in (Google, ORCID or your institution work), and under API Key Management press Create an API key.
Create a key at dev.elsevier.com: sign in with an Elsevier account (your university email works best), press Create API Key, give it a name and accept the terms.
Elsevier gives this to institutions, not to people: ask your library, which can request one from Elsevier for your API key. Without it, Scopus works on your institution’s network only.
Open the Web of Science Starter API: sign in, register an application with this API (the Free Trial plan: 50 searches a day; your institution may offer more), and copy its key.
Register at developer.ieee.org and ask for a key for the Metadata Search API. It is free; turning it on can take a few days.
Fill in Semantic Scholar’s request form: it is free, and the key comes by email.
Turn on the Books API in Google Cloud: make a project, press Enable, then under Credentials choose Create credentials › API key, and copy it.
Create a key at zotero.org: sign in, leave “Allow library access” and “Allow write access” ticked, press Save Key and copy the key it shows.
Get a key at openalex.org: make a free account and copy the key the page shows.

Enter your library's proxy address so paywalled papers your institution subscribes to open with your access.
How to find it: While off-campus, open any journal article through your library's website. Once it loads, look at the browser address bar — your library's proxy host appears in front of the publisher's address. Examples of what to paste:
  • a host like https://ezp.biblio.unitn.it
  • or a login prefix ending in ?url=, e.g. https://uni.idm.oclc.org/login?url=
If unsure, ask your library for the “EZProxy URL.”
With it, books and chapters get a Get it at button: your library's own page for the book, which says whether it has the book or the e-book and lets you request it or ask for an interlibrary loan. Libraries call it their OpenURL or link resolver address; ask yours if unsure. Left empty, there is no button.
